Start here: 3 live tickets

These are written the way a client or boss actually reports them. Symptoms only. Fix each one in your Playground copy, then check yourself against the fix path under it (try first, peek second).

Ticket 1 - "Our promo code doesn't work"

Coupons A customer says MOVEDAY10 gives an error at checkout and the sale ends this week. The owner swears the coupon was set up right.

Fix path: WooCommerce > Coupons > MOVEDAY10. Check the expiry date before anything else - it expired yesterday. Coupons fail silently into a generic "coupon is not valid" message, so expiry, usage limits, and minimum spend are the first three checks, in that order. Extend the date and test at checkout with a real cart.

Ticket 2 - "Customers can't buy the X-Large box"

Variable products On "Moving Box - Choose Your Size," Small through Large add to cart fine. X-Large shows no price and cannot be purchased. The product page itself looks normal.

Fix path: Products > Moving Box > Variations > X-Large. Its price field is empty. A variation with no price is treated as not purchasable, and WooCommerce does not flag it anywhere obvious - you find it by opening the variation. Set $7.99, save, and confirm the front end now shows a price when X-Large is selected.

Ticket 3 - "The tape fell off the website"

Visibility Packing Tape - 6 Pack exists in the product list and its direct link works, but it does not appear in the shop or its category, and search can't find it.

Fix path: Products > Packing Tape > Catalog visibility (right sidebar in the editor). It is set to Hidden. Hidden = direct link only, excluded from shop, category, and search. Set it back to "Shop and search results." The direct link working is the tell: deleted or draft products 404, hidden ones still load.

Next drills (do in Playground, break it freely)

Drill 4 - Shipping math

Build a Texas cart at $74.99 and one at $75.00. Watch the free-shipping method appear exactly at the threshold, and the flat rate disappear. Then change the threshold to $50 in the zone settings and re-test. This is how you verify a "free shipping is broken" complaint instead of guessing.

Drill 5 - Order lifecycle

Take order #28 (on-hold, bank transfer). Mark the payment received the way a store does: change status to Processing, add an order note, then complete it. Watch stock levels and the customer email log change at each step.

Drill 6 - "Store emails aren't sending"

The master lab's server has no mail transport, so order emails fail silently - the most common WooCommerce ticket there is. In Playground, install a mail-logging plugin and place a test order to see what the log proves. On a real store the fix is an SMTP plugin with an authenticated domain, and the log is how you prove it works.

Drill 7 - Sale scheduling

The Bubble Cushion Roll is on sale with no end date. Set a sale schedule (dates in the product's Pricing section), then schedule a future sale on the Wardrobe Box and confirm it does NOT show early.

Drill 8 - Low stock and backorders

The Furniture Blanket has 3 left (low-stock threshold is 5). Find where WooCommerce flags it in admin. Then place the Dish Pack (stock 0, backorders on) in a cart and see how the storefront warns the customer before checkout, not after.

Drill 9 - Kill the store, then revive it

Deactivate WooCommerce, look at the site, reactivate. Then activate a second page-builder plugin and check the shop layout. Plugin-conflict isolation - deactivate half, test, repeat - is the core troubleshooting method for "the update broke my store."

Drill 10 - System status before opinions

WooCommerce > Status. Read the whole page: PHP version, memory limit, database, active plugins, theme. Pros check this page before touching anything. The build log for this lab (in the lab folder) shows 7 real issues diagnosed this way.

The WooCommerce map (what the job actually is)

  • Products are 4 jobs: simple, variable (attributes + variations), grouped, virtual/downloadable. If you can build all four, you can build any catalog.
  • Money has 3 layers: tax rules, shipping zones/methods, payment gateways. Most "checkout is broken" tickets live in one of these three.
  • Orders are a pipeline: Pending > On-hold / Processing > Completed, with Failed and Refunded off to the side. Status changes drive emails and stock.
  • Coupons fail for boring reasons: expiry, usage limits, min/max spend, product exclusions - in that order.
  • Visibility has 4 states: published, draft, private, hidden catalog. A product can be "gone" without being deleted.
  • Status page first. WooCommerce > Status and Tools (transients, logs, recount stock) before any deep debugging.
